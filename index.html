Building a data platform from zero, then leading its next architecture

I was Aspire's first data hire. There was no warehouse, no ingestion, no shared model of the business - just databases scattered across MySQL, PostgreSQL, MSSQL, and a pile of SFTP reports. Almost four years later I'm leading the platform that replaced all of that, now serving finance, ops, and leadership across four regions.

How it evolved - three roles, one platform

Founding Data Engineer Dec 2022 – Mar 2024

  • Designed Aspire's first data platform on AWS (Redshift, MWAA, S3) and a Medallion architecture in dbt, replacing ad-hoc spreadsheet reporting with clean, analytics-ready tables.
  • Built the ETL/ELT layer consolidating MySQL, PostgreSQL, MSSQL and SFTP sources, then closed the loop with Reverse ETL (Hightouch) so sales, marketing and ops got warehouse data back into their own tools.
  • Adopted Airbyte OSS to integrate 20+ third-party apps, CRMs and productivity tools (Google Sheets, HubSpot, Stripe, and others) without hand-writing a connector for each one.
  • Put automated data-quality checks behind CI/CD (GitHub Actions), SQL validation and alerting, and locked access down with IAM-based role management.

Senior Data Engineer Mar 2024 – Mar 2026

  • Redesigned the warehouse around Databricks, Spectrum and S3 - cutting AWS spend by over 40% and pipeline processing time by 52%.
  • Named MVP of the Year 2024 across the finance cluster for that cost/architecture work.
  • Standardized data models and automated dashboard refreshes, lifting self-reported reporting speed by 25% and cutting ad-hoc data requests by over 30%.
  • Mentored junior engineers toward owning production pipelines independently, and introduced structured code review, deployment, and incident-handling practices.

Lead Data Engineer Apr 2026 – present

  • Leading the platform's evolution across AWS to support multi-region operations as Aspire expands beyond Singapore and Hong Kong into the US and Netherlands.
  • Integrated Claude with production data systems over the MCP protocol - real-time natural-language querying across AWS, Tableau and Airbyte, cutting time spent on ad-hoc data investigation.
  • Building out governance for a multi-region business: Attribute-Based Access Control, column-level masking, Unity Catalog, and PII classification.

Tata Communications - Feasibility & Pricing, Data Science Team · 2019–2021

Checking last-mile feasibility, at scale

Optimus, Tata Communications' feasibility-and-pricing engine, needed to work out whether a new last-mile connectivity request could actually be served - and from where - before any physical work started. That meant running geo-spatial analysis on network data to answer the feasibility question, and I built the data layer underneath it.

  • Built the ETL that transformed geo-spatial data to find the accurate base station for a connectivity request, saving the organization roughly ₹2.5 million.
  • Built flow-based Flask APIs in Python and optimized the engine with vectorized operations, improving performance by about 40%.
  • Raised the feasibility engine's zero-touch rate - requests resolved without manual review - by about 12.5% within a year.
  • Moved deployment to a Docker–Jenkins pipeline, cutting manual deployment effort by roughly 60%.

About

A bit more about me.

I studied Computer Science at SASTRA University (NIRF Top 25 for CS), and my first real project wasn't a web app - it was cleaning historical medical survey data as a research assistant and running classifiers against it until the predictions held up. That published paper is still the clearest example I have of why I trust pipelines more than models: a good algorithm fed bad data fails faster than a mediocre algorithm fed good data.

That instinct followed me into industry. At Tata Communications I built the data layer under Optimus, the feasibility-and-pricing engine that worked out whether a new last-mile connectivity request could be served and from where; at Accern I optimized a predictive-modeling backend; and for almost four years now at Aspire I've gone from being the first data hire to leading the architecture, governance, and AI-integrated access layer for a platform running across four countries. I like the unglamorous parts most - the ingestion job that doesn't need babysitting, the access model that survives an audit, the migration that cuts the AWS bill without anyone noticing a regression.

Outside of Aspire, I write occasionally on Medium and keep experimenting with whatever's next in the data-and-AI overlap - right now that's mostly MCP-based, natural-language access to production data.
